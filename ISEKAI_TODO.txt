LEXIA - ISEKAI TODO
The isekai features to add to the game
==========================================================

This file adds to GAME_OBJECTIVE.txt and REALISM.txt.
All the numbers are first proposals, to balance after tests.
Priority: (A) first, (B) then, (C) later.
The golden rules of REALISM.txt (part 1) still apply: English first, never in a question,
one mechanic = one file with clear function names.


==========================================================
1. THE GUILD AND THE ADVENTURERS
==========================================================

(A) Promotion exam
  - To go up a guild rank, the hero must pass an exam at the guild.
  - The exam is a real mock TOEIC (reading part): a timed series of questions, the score at
    the end, and the rank given if the score is high enough.
  - Higher rank = harder exam (more RC 55 texts, less time).
  - A failed exam can be taken again the next day. The receptionist gives the result.
  - The result goes into the app's stats, like every other answer.

(A) Adventurer card
  - A real item in the inventory: the name, the rank (F to S), the level, the class, the
    number of monsters defeated, the title.
  - It updates by itself. Showing it to a guard or a merchant can open doors or give discounts.
  - It has a picture in the inventory, with a color for each rank (bronze, silver, gold...).

(A) The guild receptionist
  - A named character at the counter of each guild (with a portrait and expressions).
  - She registers the hero, gives the quests, the exams and the rewards.
  - She reacts to the exploits: shocked ("T-this is impossible! A rank F defeated a drake?!"),
    proud, worried when the hero comes back hurt.
  - She remembers the hero: her lines change with the rank and the fame.

(B) Found your own guild
  - At a high rank, the hero can found a guild: a name, a banner, a building.
  - Recruit members (villagers, friends of the party, intelligent monsters, see part 4).
  - Send members on quests: they come back with Lexis, items or wounds.
  - The guild levels up: more members, a bigger building, rooms (see part 7).


==========================================================
2. PROGRESSION
==========================================================

(A) Skill tree
  - Each level up gives skill points.
  - The tree has branches (Sword, Magic, Survival, Words...). Each node = a small bonus
    or a new ability.
  - Some nodes need a mastery of a grammar category to open (e.g. "Tenses Lv.40").

(A) Achievements
  - A list of achievements with an icon: "First blood", "100 gerunds without a mistake",
    "Survived a raid", "Answered in less than 3 seconds"...
  - Each one is shown in a popup when earned, and in a new tab of the menu.
  - Some give a small reward (Lexis, a title, a cosmetic).

(B) Weapon mastery
  - Each type of weapon (sword, bow, staff, dagger...) has its own level, earned by using it.
  - A higher mastery: more damage, more critical hits, a special move at some levels.

(B) Mana exhaustion
  - Using all the mana makes the hero faint (a black screen, they wake up some hours later).
  - But each exhaustion raises the max mana a little (+1), like the reborn babies of isekai.
  - Always warned: a sign on the HUD when the mana is very low.


==========================================================
3. MAGIC
==========================================================

(A) Written incantations
  - Some spells are cast by writing or completing an English sentence (the incantation).
  - A longer and correct sentence = a stronger spell. A mistake = the spell fails.
  - Example: "Flames that burned the ancient cities, ______ my enemy!" (consume / consumed /
    consuming / to consume).
  - Out of fights only, or for the big spells (rule: never slow a fight question).
  - Later, the hero can cast some spells without the incantation (silent casting).

(B) Time magic
  - Slow the timer of a question (more time to answer).
  - Stop the time for a few seconds in the world (the monsters freeze).
  - A rare and expensive magic, learned late in the story.

(B) Gravity magic
  - Make a monster heavier (it loses speed, so the hero has more time).
  - Levitate: cross a river or a hole, reach a high place.
  - Throw rocks or crush a group of small monsters.

(B) Contracts with spirits
  - 4 elemental spirits: Salamander (fire), Undine (water), Sylph (wind), Gnome (earth).
  - Each one lives in a hidden place. To sign the contract: a test of words (a text to read
    and questions about it).
  - A spirit gives a power and can be called in fights. It talks with the hero and levels up.


==========================================================
4. MONSTERS
==========================================================

(B) Tame a monster
  - A monster defeated without a single mistake can ask to follow the hero.
  - It fights with the party, has a name and levels up.
  - It can live at home or in the guild when it is not in the party.

(B) Flying mount
  - A griffin or a wyvern, earned in a quest (an egg to hatch, or a monster to tame).
  - It flies over the map: rivers, mountains and sea are no longer a problem.
  - It can only land in open places (not in forests or inside towns).

(B) Intelligent monsters
  - Some monsters talk: a peaceful goblin village, a wise lizardman, a lost harpy.
  - The hero can talk, trade and make peace with them instead of fighting.
  - They can be recruited (party, guild) and they have their own quests.

(B) Monster ranks
  - Each monster has a rank from F to S (shown above it, with a color).
  - The rank matches the difficulty of its questions and its stats.
  - A guild quest says the rank of the monster to defeat.

(B) World bosses: the emissaries and clans of the Demon King
  - Rare great enemies that travel on the map: the emissaries of the Demon King, or whole
    clans of demons (a chief and their soldiers).
  - They are announced: a message of the guild, villagers who flee, a dark sky.
  - They attack a region (villages, caravans) until the hero stops them.
  - Big rewards, a title, and the Demon King loses part of his army.


==========================================================
5. DUNGEONS
==========================================================

(B) Dungeon towers
  - Towers to climb, floor by floor (around 10 to 15 floors, not 100).
  - A boss every 5 floors. A checkpoint after each boss.
  - The questions get harder at each floor.

(B) Secret floors
  - A hidden floor in some dungeons and towers: a hidden door, a clue to read in English
    (a book, a note on a wall).
  - A hidden boss and a rare treasure.

(C) Group raid
  - A big dungeon or a world boss attacked with a group of NPC adventurers.
  - Roles: tank (takes the hits), healer, damage. The hero leads the group.
  - The good answers of the hero give orders to the group.


==========================================================
6. EQUIPMENT
==========================================================

(B) Refining from +1 to +10
  - At the smith: pay Lexis and materials to refine a weapon or an armor.
  - Each level gives more damage or defense. "Iron Sword +5".
  - The higher the level, the higher the risk of failing (the level goes down, never the item
    lost, rule "always a way out").
  - A protection scroll prevents the loss of a level.


==========================================================
7. HOUSES AND BUILDINGS
==========================================================

(A) More light inside the houses
  - Candles, lamps, the fire of the fireplace with a warm glow (draw/lights.js does it only
    outside today).
  - The light of the windows by day, darker at night.
  - Light sources the hero can buy for their home.

(A) Bedrooms
  - In the hero's house: a real bedroom (today home.js has only one bed in the room).
    More bedrooms when the family or the party grows.
  - In the guild hall: rooms upstairs to rent for the night (the guild is only one room today).
  - The rooms of the guild of the hero (see part 1) for its members.


==========================================================
8. PEOPLE
==========================================================

(B) Personal quests of the companions
  - Each friend of the party has their own story arc: their past, a family, an enemy.
  - The end of the arc unlocks their ultimate power and a big bond with the hero.

(B) Letters
  - Villagers, friends, the receptionist and the kingdoms send letters to the hero.
  - A letter is a text in English (like the emails of the TOEIC part 6).
  - Some ask a question or need an answer: choose the right reply (a TOEIC question).
  - A letterbox at home, a messenger who brings urgent letters.


==========================================================
9. PRESENTATION
==========================================================

(A) Level up screen
  - A big screen when the hero levels up: the new level, then the stats go up one by one
    with a sound for each.
  - The new skill points (see part 2), the new abilities.

(C) Anime episode style
  - Each chapter is cut into episodes, with a title shown at the start
    ("Episode 3: The slime who didn't know the past tense").
  - An opening scene for the first episode of a chapter.
  - "To be continued..." and a short "Next time" at the end of an episode.

(C) Gallery and photo album
  - A gallery of illustrations, unlocked at the big moments of the story.
  - A photo album: the game takes a picture at the key moments (first victory, wedding,
    a world boss defeated...). The hero can also take pictures.

(C) Weekly challenge (solo)
  - Each week, a challenge for the player: answer 50 questions of a category, defeat
    a monster in less than X seconds, clear a tower without a potion...
  - The game is solo: the player is ranked against their own records (best week, best time,
    longest series), not against other players.
  - A reward each week the challenge is done.
