LIFE: 100 IDEAS TO MAKE THE WORLD FEEL ALIVE
============================================

A living world: the world goes on without the hero. People have a day, nature moves, the war changes the land,
and the hero sees it, hears it, and can step in. (Already in the game, not listed here: the routines of the
villagers, their small talk, the caravans, the demon land and the forest fires, the raids, the weather, the
seasons, the families.)

Each idea: what the player SEES or HEARS. A system nobody can see is not worth building.
The best first ones (a lot of life for little work): 5, 26, 33, 61, 89.


1. THE PEOPLE
-------------
1. They react to the hero: a friend says the hero's name, a villager whispers if the hero has a bad name,
   people step aside when the hero runs with a weapon out.
2. They react to the world: they run under a roof when it rains, form a bucket chain when a forest burns,
   close their shutters during a raid.
3. They have needs we can see: lunch at the inn at noon, water from the well in the morning, bed when tired.
4. They remember each other: two neighbors who fought avoid each other, two friends meet on the square at night.
5. Their jobs can be seen and heard: the smith hits the anvil (sparks, noise), the baker takes the bread out in
   the morning (smoke), the fisherman walks to the lake with his rod.
6. Kids play: tag, chasing the chickens, throwing stones in the river, following the hero around.
7. Pets: cats on the roofs, dogs that follow their owner and bark at monsters.
8. Moods on the face: a sad villager after a death in the family, a happy one on a wedding day.
9. Old people sit on a bench in front of their house and watch the street.
10. Lovers walk hand in hand by the river at sunset.
11. Couples argue in front of their door (a short scene, raised voices, then one of them leaves).
12. People carry things: a sack of flour, a bucket, firewood, a basket of fish.
13. People get sick: they stay home, a healer visits them, they come out again a few days later.
14. People get old: gray hair after many days, and one day an old villager is not there anymore.
15. A newcomer moves into an empty house, with a cart full of furniture.
16. People leave a village that is too dangerous (near the demon land), and their house stays empty.
17. Apprentices: a young villager follows the smith for a few days, then works alone.
18. People pray at the church on the day of the temple, and the bell rings for them.
19. Drunk people sing on the way home after the inn.
20. Gossip travels: what the hero did in one village is told in the next one a day later.


2. THE VILLAGES
---------------
21. Weekly market: stalls on the square, merchants shouting their prices, more people than usual.
22. Weddings: a procession through the streets, music, flowers, a feast on the square.
23. Funerals: a silent procession to the cemetery, a new grave stays there.
24. Births: the church bell rings, the neighbors bring gifts to the house.
25. Buildings grow: a house under construction, with scaffolding that rises day after day, then a family moves in.
26. The evening: a lamplighter lights the lanterns one by one, music at the inn, windows light up.
27. Guards change shifts at a fixed hour, and walk their rounds along the walls.
28. Crimes between people: a pickpocket robs a villager who shouts "Thief!", and the hero can step in.
29. A town crier reads the news of the day on the square every morning.
30. Laundry day: clothes drying on lines between the houses, washerwomen at the river.
31. The well is a meeting place: in the morning, a small line of people with buckets.
32. Repairs: after a raid, builders fix the broken walls and roofs, tile after tile.
33. Smoke from the chimneys: more in the morning and the evening, more in winter, none in an empty house.
34. Shop signs that open and close: "OPEN" in the morning, shutters closed at night.
35. A school: kids walk to school in the morning and run out at noon.
36. A village festival once a season: decorations, games, a contest the hero can join.
37. A fire in a house (a candle, lightning): villagers shout and bring water.
38. Beggars at the gates of the capitals, who thank the hero for a coin.
39. Street musicians and jugglers on the squares of the capitals.
40. Village councils: the elder and a few villagers argue at a table about the walls, the taxes, the demons.


3. NATURE
---------
41. Wild animals with a behavior: deer that run away, wolves in packs at night, birds that fly off when the hero
    passes, fish that jump in the lakes.
42. A food chain: wolves hunt deer; too many rabbits eat the fields; foxes steal chickens at night.
43. Tracks and signs: footprints in the snow and the mud, a carcass left by a hunter, nests in the trees.
44. Nature reacts to the weather: puddles after the rain, rivers that overflow, leaves that fall in autumn,
    snow that covers everything in winter.
45. Plants live: fields grow, are harvested, then are bare; fruit trees bloom in spring and give fruit in summer.
46. Flocks of birds cross the sky (shadows on the ground), and go south in autumn.
47. Butterflies in the meadows by day, fireflies at night in summer.
48. Frogs croak near the ponds after the rain.
49. Bees around the hives and the flowers.
50. Wind in the trees: leaves and grass move when the wind is strong.
51. Storms leave marks: a fallen tree on the road, a broken fence, a flooded field.
52. Animals have babies in spring: fawns follow their mother, chicks follow the hen.
53. Migrations: herds of bison cross the plains once a season.
54. Mushrooms grow after the rain, in the forests.
55. The sea moves: waves on the beach, the tide goes up and down, seaweed on the sand.
56. Lightning can set a tree on fire in a storm (not only the demons).
57. Burnt forests grow back step by step: ash, then grass, then small trees, then the forest.
58. Day and night creatures: owls and bats come out at sunset, roosters sing at dawn.
59. Hunters set traps in the forest, and come back to check them.
60. A rare animal that few people saw (a white stag): a rumor, then a sighting.


4. THE WORLD AT WAR
-------------------
61. Refugees: when the demons take land, families flee on the roads with their carts, toward the towns.
62. Villages near the demon land fortify: palisades, watchtowers, more guards.
63. Messengers: a rider crosses the land to bring news (a town fell, a boss is dead), then people talk about it.
64. Ruins: a village taken by the demons stays burnt; the hero can free it and watch it being rebuilt.
65. Smoke on the horizon where a battle or a fire is happening.
66. Soldiers march from the capital to the front, singing.
67. Wounded soldiers come back to the villages, with bandages, and rest at the clinic.
68. War orphans in the capitals.
69. Prices go up near the front (less food), and down when the land is freed.
70. Deserters hide in the forests, and may turn into bandits.
71. Statues and memorials for the heroes of the war, built in the squares after a victory.
72. Black banners on the houses of the families who lost someone.
73. Scouts watch the demon land from the hills, and run to warn the village when demons come close.
74. The demon land changes the ground: gray grass, dead trees, a red sky near it.
75. Freed land heals slowly: the gray goes away over a few days.


5. THE ROADS
------------
76. Random events: an overturned cart to help, a peddler, a pilgrim who asks the way (in English: a TOEIC
    question), a duel between two adventurers.
77. Rival adventurers who take the same quests, met in a dungeon or at the inn.
78. Campfires of travelers at night, where the hero can sit and hear rumors.
79. Farmers drive their cows and sheep along the roads to the market.
80. A broken bridge that workers repair over a few days (the road is closed meanwhile).
81. Milestones and signposts that tell the distance to the next town.
82. Inns along the roads, with stables full of horses at night.
83. Bandits who block a road, and a reward when the road is free again.
84. A traveling circus or theater troupe that goes from town to town.
85. Pilgrims walking to a shrine, all in white.
86. A lost child on the road, to bring back home.
87. Royal carriages with guards, on the way to another capital.
88. Toll gates at the borders of the kingdoms, with a guard who checks papers.


6. THE ATMOSPHERE
-----------------
89. The sounds of the world: church bells on the hour, roosters at dawn, crickets at night, the noise of the
    market, the forge far away.
90. Smells shown as small effects: bread steam at the bakery, smoke at the forge, flowers in spring.
91. Music changes with the place and the hour: lively on the market, calm at night, tense near the demon land.
92. Light changes all day: golden morning, white noon, orange evening, blue night.
93. Footsteps leave marks: a path appears in the grass where everybody walks.
94. Paths and roads get busier near the towns, and empty in the wild.
95. A planted tree grows over the days.
96. Graves appear in the cemetery when a villager dies, with their name on it.
97. The world remembers the hero: a statue after a big victory, a song at the inn about what the hero did.
98. Seasonal decorations: flowers in spring, lanterns in summer, pumpkins in autumn, lights in winter.
99. Weather in the distance: a rain curtain on the horizon, a rainbow after the rain.
100. The world goes on when the hero is away: coming back after many days, things have changed (a new house,
     a new baby, a shop that closed, a wall that was repaired).
