PATCH: WHAT THE GAME NEEDS BEFORE MORE CONTENT
==============================================

The game already has a lot (see life.txt for the living world ideas). What it needs now is that what exists
works well, stays playable, and helps the player get a better TOEIC reading score.
The order of the parts below is the order to do them in.

Not in this patch (decided): no notebook of mistakes to read again, no audio / listening part (the game stays
written only), the explanations of the mistakes stay as they are, the interface stays as it is, no intro.


1. RELIABILITY: TESTS THAT CATCH THE BUGS
-----------------------------------------
The last fixes were deep bugs found by hand: the RC 8 / RC 55 texts showing only their first document, people
stuck in front of the houses or walking into walls, demons appearing anywhere, beating "Thamuz" in the Spire
killing the real one in the castle. The code was written fast, by several AIs, and nothing checks it.

A small set of automatic tests (run with one command, before each push):
  1. Every question with a text: its text is shown whole (every document), its answers belong to it.
  2. A save is written, read back, and the game goes on the same.
  3. 10 days of game on the map without a crash (the world updated frame after frame).
  4. The people move: nobody walks without moving, nobody goes back and forth forever, nobody stands for long
     in front of a door.
  5. The demons only appear on the demon land; their generals stay on the Silent Isle.
  6. Each boss, once beaten, only removes itself (no other boss or floor disappears).
  7. Every shop opens and sells only its kind of items.


2. PROGRESS: THE TOEIC SCORE IN THE GAME
----------------------------------------
Playing must feel like getting better at the TOEIC.
  1. An estimated TOEIC reading score (5 to 495), from the answers of the game and of the app, visible in the
     game (the HUD or the stats menu).
  2. It moves after each fight: "+3" in green, "-2" in red, so the player sees that each answer counts.
  3. The goal score of the player shown next to it, with the distance left.
  4. Per category (gerunds, tenses, prepositions...): the level and the trend of the last days.


3. BALANCE: PLAY A WHOLE GAME
-----------------------------
Several numbers changed at the same time, and nobody played a whole game since:
  - the health of all the monsters doubled (bosses and demons too);
  - every level now costs the same XP (300);
  - the demon land spreads every day, and burns forests.
To check, by playing or by a simulation of a whole game:
  1. How long a normal fight lasts, now that monsters have twice the health.
  2. How fast the hero levels up, from the start to the Demon King.
  3. How far the demon land goes before the hero can fight it back (not too fast, not invisible).
  4. That nothing blocks the story (a boss too strong, a road closed, a quest that never ends).


4. THE GOAL OF THE MOMENT
-------------------------
With so many systems (guild, realism, economy, war, romance, spirits, the Spire...), the player can get lost.
  1. One line always visible: what to do now in the main story ("Defeat General Barbarism in the
     Sunscorch Desert").
  2. No tutorial, no intro: only this line, and the map mark that already exists.


5. PERFORMANCE
--------------
The more the world lives, the more it computes. Today: about 2 ms per frame.
  1. Measure it again after each big addition from life.txt, on a phone too.
  2. Only the people, animals and monsters near the hero are updated every frame; the far ones live "in numbers"
     (like the villages far away already do).
  3. A limit on the number of moving things on screen.


6. OPTIMIZATIONS
----------------
Where the game spends its time, every frame (to check with the browser profiler first, then fix the worst):
  1. The drawing of the world (draw/scene.js) makes a new small function for every tree, rock and person in
     view, every frame. Keep the drawings of what does not move, and only rebuild them when the view moves to
     another block.
  2. Many lists are filtered every frame (NPCS.filter(...), monsters near the hero, villagers near the hero).
     Filter once per frame (or once per second for the slow things) and share the result.
  3. The ensure...() functions (23 of them) check and fill the state again and again, every frame. Call them
     once when the game is loaded (save.js / newGame.js), then just read the state.
  4. zoneAt() is called a lot for the same tiles: keep the zone of each moving thing, and look again only when it
     changes tile.
  5. The searches of a way (world/paths.js) are the most expensive thing a walker does: never more than a few
     per frame for the whole world (a small queue), the others wait their turn.
  6. The forest fires and the demon land count their lists with Object.keys() often: keep a counter instead.
  7. The far villages, caravans and parties should live "in numbers" (one step per second), not frame by frame.
  8. The big pictures (the world map painting, the atlases) are loaded once, and only drawn when on screen.


7. CLEAN THE CODE
-----------------
There is no CLAUDE.md in the project yet: write one, with the rules below, so that every AI follows them.
The rules (they are already the style of the first files of the project):
  - "Be simple as fuck. And the clearest humanely readable function. One function for one functionality."
    (the first rule, above all the others)
  - As simple and readable as possible: one function = one thing, with a clear name.
  - A plain comment above each function that is not obvious: what it does and why, in simple English.
  - Small files: one subject per file. Data (lists of monsters, items, sentences) in data/, rules in logic/,
    drawing in draw/, screens in ui/.
  - Plain objects and small named functions: no classes, no clever abstractions.
  - The numbers that can be tuned are named constants at the top of the file, with a comment.

What to clean, from what was measured:
  1. Split the biggest files by subject: draw/scene.js (1879 lines), draw/props.js (1465), logic/houses.js
     (1369: the houses, the halls, the residents, the academies are four subjects).
  2. Cut the very long functions into small named ones (some are 250 to 500 lines: specialHallSpec in
     logic/houses.js, the panels of ui/menu/, dealText in ui/ShopScreen.jsx, slowNatureAndRoads in
     logic/life/natureAndRoads.js...).
  3. One helper, used everywhere, for what is written again and again: the distance between two things
     (10 copies), turning to face something (5 copies), "is the hero near".
  4. Comments: the same plain style everywhere (111 comments are in the "/** ... */" style of another AI, often
     long and full of words like "robust" or "seamless": make them simple, or remove them when the name says it).
  5. Time: 126 calls to Date.now() in the rules of the game. Use the clock of the game instead, so that the
     pause, the frozen time and the tests work the same everywhere.
  6. Remove what is never used: exported functions nobody calls, old fields (like the old _steerSign of the
     walkers), items or data that nothing reads.
  7. The same name for the same thing everywhere (people / npcs / villagers / patrons / residents: say in a
     comment which is which, and do not add new words for the same idea).
  8. After each cleaning step: the build passes, and the tests of part 1 pass.
